Type Test 1: Leakage Test (Clause 7.2.1)

Procedure
  • Test the complete tank assembly, including the fuel pick-up tube, fill pipe and fuel gauge/sender (7.1).
  • Test pressure: the greatest of 20 kPa, 1,5 × the highest hydrostatic pressure in service (maximum fill-up height above tank top), or, for a sealed fuel system, 1,5 × the maximum hydrostatic head plus the system relief pressure.
  • Hold the static pressure for 5 min with no drop or rise.
  • Check for leakage with a method other than pressure drop: non-corrosive, non-toxic soap solution or full immersion in water. Do not use solutions containing ammonia.
Pass criteria
  • No pressure drop or rise during the 5 min hold, and no leakage found.

Type Test 2: Pressure/Strength Test (Clause 7.2.2)

Fibre-reinforced tanks: Clause 7.2.2.2, held for 5 min
  • After the leakage test, pressurise the tank again with all its accessories.
  • Increase the pressure gradually to the greatest of 20 kPa, 1,5 × the highest hydrostatic pressure in service (maximum fill-up or overflow height above tank top), or, for a sealed system, 1,5 × the maximum hydrostatic head plus the relief pressure.
  • Hold for 5 min.
Thermoplastic tanks: Clause 7.2.2.4, held for 60 min (high density) or 5 h (low density)
  • Before the test, fill the tank and store it for at least 28 days at an ambient temperature of at least 21 °C, with the fuel the tank is made for, liquid C test fuel blended with 10 % ± 1 % ethanol, or CE10 fuel.
  • Empty the tank and test it immediately.
  • Increase the pressure gradually to the greatest of 20 kPa, 1,5 × the highest hydrostatic pressure in service, or, for a sealed system, 1,5 × the maximum hydrostatic head plus the relief pressure.
  • Hold for the time given in Table 2: 60 min for high density, 5 h for low density.
Pass criteria
  • The tank does not crack or leak during the hold. Permanent deformation is allowed.

Type Test 3: Pressure-Impulse Test (Clause 7.3)

Procedure
  • Use a test tank representative of the tank range. Thermoplastic tanks are prepared as for 7.2.2.4 (28 days fuel conditioning).
  • Before the test, the tank passes the leakage test of 7.2.1.
  • Fill the tank fully with water and mount it on the supports, chocks or brackets supplied with it or intended for the installation.
  • Connect a regulated source of air, nitrogen or water. Cycle the pressure, measured at the top of the tank, from 0 to 20 kPa and back to 0 kPa, at no more than 15 cycles per minute.
  • Run 25 000 cycles, then repeat the leakage test of 7.2.1.
Pass criteria
  • No leakage or other sign of failure after 25 000 cycles, and the leakage test passed before and after.

Type Test 4: Fire-Resistance Test (Clause 7.4 or 7.5)

7.4 General fire-resistance test
  • Tanks tested per range: the smallest, the largest, and tanks in between at intervals of no more than 33 % by volume compared with the smallest. A range has the same material, method, wall thickness, a similar shape and number of surfaces, a similar configuration, and a defined maximum volume-to-baffle-spacing ratio if baffled.
  • The tank first passes the pressure tests of 7.2.
  • Support the tank at its ends and at each baffle in a fire-resistant enclosure open on one side only. Clearances: at least 50 mm at the sides, at least 150 mm at the ends, 175 mm to 850 mm at the top.
  • Lowest point of the tank 75 mm above the heptane surface. The heptane pan extends 50 mm beyond the sides and 150 mm beyond the ends, and holds enough heptane to burn for 2,5 min.
  • Fill the tank to 25 % of its capacity with the intended fuel. Cap all openings except the vents, which are led outside the fire area.
  • Burn for 2,5 min in a draught-free area with free air inflow. The temperature reaches at least 650 °C within 25 mm of the tank surface at some time during the test. Extinguish at 2,5 min.
  • After cooling, drain the tank and pressurise it slowly with air or inert gas up to 1,8 kPa.
7.5 Fire-resistance test as installed
  • For a tank of known construction in a specified or controlled installation, in a particular craft or series.
  • Install the tank in an actual or simulated hull section large enough to represent the fire conditions, made of the craft's materials or materials of equivalent flammability. For a series, use the section giving the maximum fire exposure.
  • The tank first passes the pressure tests of 7.2, then the pressure is released.
  • Fill the tank to 25 % of its capacity with the intended fuel. Cap all openings except the vents, which are led outside the fire area.
  • Pour heptane into every crevice and liquid trap where leaked fuel could collect, enough to burn for 2,5 min.
  • Burn for 2,5 min in a draught-free area with free air inflow, reaching at least 650 °C within 25 mm of the tank surface. Extinguish at 2,5 min.
  • After cooling, drain the tank and pressurise it slowly with air or inert gas up to 1,8 kPa.
Pass criteria
  • After the fire and the 1,8 kPa pressure test, the tank shows no leakage as defined in 7.2.1.
