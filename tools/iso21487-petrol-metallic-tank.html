Project Information

About Type Tests: Type tests are laboratory tests performed on representative tank samples to verify that the tank design meets all safety and performance requirements of ISO 21487:2022. These tests need only be conducted once for each tank design or model range. Once a tank design has successfully passed type testing, individual production tanks of that design only require production quality tests (leakage test), not the full type test series.

5.2 Type Tests for Petrol Metallic Tanks (Table 2)

Type Test Overview: Per ISO 21487:2022 Table 2, metallic petrol fuel tanks must undergo THREE mandatory type tests:
  • Test 1: Leakage Test (Clause 7.2.1)
  • Test 2: Pressure/Strength Test (Clause 7.2.2)
  • Test 3: Pressure-Impulse Test (Clause 7.3)
Fire testing (Clauses 7.4 and 7.5) is NOT required for metallic tanks.

Type Test 1: Leakage Test (Clause 7.2.1)

Test Purpose and Objective

The leakage test verifies the integrity of all tank seams, welds, fittings, and connections under normal operating pressure conditions. This test ensures that the tank will not leak fuel during normal service use.

Test Procedure

Step 1: Test Setup

  • Tank must be tested with all fittings, connections, and closures installed as they would be in service
  • Tank must be mounted or supported to prevent distortion during testing
  • All openings except the test pressure connection must be sealed

Step 2: Pressure Calculation

Calculate the required test pressure as the GREATER of:

  • 20 kPa (minimum absolute value)
  • 1.5 times the highest hydrostatic pressure to which the tank will be subjected in normal service
  • For sealed systems: 1.5 times (maximum hydrostatic head + system relief valve pressure)

Step 3: Pressurization Method

  • Use clean, dry air or inert gas (nitrogen recommended)
  • Gradually increase pressure to the calculated test pressure
  • Avoid sudden pressure application that could damage the tank

Step 4: Static Pressure Hold

  • Maintain the test pressure for a minimum of 5 minutes
  • Monitor pressure gauge to ensure no pressure drop or rise occurs
  • Record initial and final pressure readings

Step 5: Leak Detection

  • Visual inspection: Look for bubbles using leak detection solution (soapy water) applied to all welds, seams, fittings, and connections
  • Alternative methods: Ultrasonic leak detector, electronic gas detector
  • DO NOT rely solely on pressure drop as the only leak detection method
Pass Criteria
  • No visible leakage at any point during the 5-minute test period
  • No pressure drop or rise during the static pressure hold period
  • No bubbles or leakage detected at welds, seams, or fittings
  • Tank structure shows no signs of distortion or damage
Failure Indicators
  • Visible fuel or test gas leakage at any location
  • Continuous pressure drop indicating a leak
  • Bubbles forming at welds, seams, or connections
  • Structural deformation or damage to tank

Type Test 2: Pressure/Strength Test (Clause 7.2.2)

Test Purpose and Objective

The pressure/strength test verifies that the tank structure can withstand pressures significantly higher than normal operating conditions without cracking or catastrophic failure. This test ensures adequate safety margins and structural integrity. Permanent deformation is acceptable, but the tank must not crack or leak.

Test Method Selection

There are TWO acceptable test methods per ISO 21487:2022:

  • Method 7.2.2.2 (Standard Method): Suitable for all metallic tanks
  • Method 7.2.2.3 (Alternate Method): Only for tanks meeting specific structural and welding quality requirements
Method 7.2.2.2 - Standard Pressure Test

Applicability: All metallic petrol fuel tanks

Test Pressure Calculation:

Test pressure must be the GREATER of:

  • 20 kPa (minimum absolute value)
  • 1.5 times the maximum hydrostatic pressure in service
  • For sealed systems: 1.5 times (maximum hydrostatic head + system relief valve pressure)

Test Procedure:

  • Conduct AFTER successful completion of leakage test (7.2.1)
  • Tank may be partially or fully filled with water to simulate service conditions
  • Apply calculated test pressure using compressed air or inert gas
  • Maintain test pressure for minimum 5 minutes
  • Monitor for any cracks, ruptures, or leakage
  • Permanent deformation is acceptable
Method 7.2.2.3 - Alternate Method (Qualifying Tanks Only)

Applicability Requirements:

This method may ONLY be used if ALL the following conditions are met:

  • Tank structure complies with ISO 12215-5:2019 (plating thickness, section modulus, web shear areas as for integral tanks)
  • Tank structural details follow ISO 12215-6:2008 (metal construction guidelines)
  • Welding quality meets minimum Class B per ISO 5817
  • All welders are qualified per ISO 9606 series or equivalent recognized standards
  • Complete technical drawings available showing material grades, thicknesses, dimensions, welding details

Test Pressure Calculation:

Test pressure must be the GREATER of:

  • 30 kPa (minimum absolute value - note this is HIGHER than standard method)
  • 1.5 times the maximum hydrostatic pressure in service + 10 kPa

Test Procedure:

  • Same procedure as Method 7.2.2.2
  • Apply calculated test pressure for minimum 5 minutes
  • Monitor for cracks, ruptures, or leakage
  • Permanent deformation is acceptable
Pass Criteria (Both Methods)
  • No cracks develop anywhere in the tank structure
  • No rupture or catastrophic failure occurs
  • No fuel leakage detected during or after test
  • Tank maintains pressure for full 5-minute duration
  • Permanent deformation is acceptable (tank does not need to return to original shape)
Failure Indicators
  • Any crack formation in welds, seams, or base material
  • Rupture or catastrophic structural failure
  • Leakage of test medium
  • Inability to maintain test pressure for 5 minutes
Important Note on Alternate Method 7.2.2.3:

Before selecting the alternate method, verify that your tank design meets ALL structural and welding requirements. If you are unsure, use the standard method 7.2.2.2. Using the alternate method without meeting all requirements invalidates the test results.

Type Test 3: Pressure-Impulse Test (Clause 7.3)

Test Purpose and Objective

The pressure-impulse test (also known as fatigue testing or cyclic pressure testing) simulates the effects of long-term pressure cycling that occurs during normal vessel operation. Pressure fluctuations in the fuel system occur due to engine operation, fuel pump cycling, wave action, and thermal expansion/contraction. This test ensures the tank can withstand these repeated pressure cycles over its service life without developing cracks or leaks.

Test Scope and Applicability

Important: This test is performed on a REPRESENTATIVE tank sample, not on every production tank. Once a tank design has passed the pressure-impulse test, all tanks manufactured to that same design are considered qualified.

Representative Sample: The test tank must represent the tank range/family in terms of:

  • Materials and material thickness
  • Manufacturing methods and welding techniques
  • Overall design and geometry
  • Critical stress areas and structural details
Pre-Test Requirements

Before beginning the pressure-impulse test:

  • Tank must FIRST pass the leakage test per Clause 7.2.1
  • Record baseline leakage test results for comparison
  • Inspect tank thoroughly for any pre-existing defects
  • Document tank condition with photographs
Test Setup and Configuration

Tank Mounting:

  • Tank must be installed using its normal support system (brackets, chocks, hangers)
  • Mounting must replicate actual installation conditions
  • Tank must be able to move and flex as it would in service
  • Do not over-constrain or rigidly fix the tank in unnatural ways

Tank Filling:

  • Tank must be COMPLETELY filled with clean water (100% full)
  • No air pockets should remain in the tank
  • All air must be bled from the system before testing
  • Temperature of water should be recorded
Test Parameters and Procedure

Pressure Cycle Specifications:

  • Cycle from: 0 kPa → 20 kPa → 0 kPa
  • Each complete cycle consists of: pressurization + hold + depressurization
  • Total number of cycles: 25,000 complete cycles
  • Cycling rate: Maximum 15 cycles per minute (to prevent overheating)
  • Test medium: Water (tank must remain fully filled throughout)

Cycle Pattern:

  • Ramp UP from 0 kPa to 20 kPa
  • Brief hold at 20 kPa
  • Ramp DOWN from 20 kPa to 0 kPa
  • Brief period at 0 kPa before next cycle begins
  • Pattern repeats 25,000 times

Test Duration:

  • At maximum rate (15 cycles/min): approximately 28 hours of continuous testing
  • More typically: 30-40 hours depending on actual cycling rate
  • Test may be paused for inspections but total cycle count must be maintained
Monitoring During Test

Throughout the 25,000 cycle test, monitor and record:

  • Pressure readings (peak and valley of each cycle)
  • Water temperature (should not exceed safe limits)
  • Visual inspection at regular intervals (every 5,000-10,000 cycles recommended)
  • Any unusual sounds, vibrations, or tank movements
  • Signs of fatigue crack initiation or propagation
  • Leakage at welds, seams, or fittings
Post-Test Inspection

After completing 25,000 cycles:

  • Drain tank and allow to dry
  • Conduct thorough visual inspection of all welds and seams
  • Use dye penetrant or magnetic particle inspection for crack detection
  • Perform complete leakage test per Clause 7.2.1 (same as pre-test)
  • Compare post-test condition to pre-test baseline
  • Document any changes in tank condition
Pass Criteria

Tank passes the pressure-impulse test if ALL the following conditions are met:

  • No leakage detected during the 25,000 cycle test
  • No structural failure or rupture occurs
  • Tank passes post-test leakage test (7.2.1) with same results as pre-test
  • No cracks detected in visual or non-destructive inspection
  • No separation of seams or fittings
  • Tank remains structurally sound throughout testing
Failure Indicators
  • Any leakage at any point during the 25,000 cycles
  • Fatigue crack development (especially at welds or stress concentrations)
  • Separation of welded joints or fittings
  • Rupture or catastrophic failure
  • Failure of post-test leakage test (7.2.1)
  • Inability to maintain pressure cycling due to tank failure
Important Testing Notes:
  • This test takes several days to complete and requires specialized pressure cycling equipment
  • Water temperature must be monitored to prevent overheating from rapid cycling
  • Test should be conducted in a controlled environment with safety barriers
  • Tank should be monitored continuously or with automatic shutdown on failure detection
